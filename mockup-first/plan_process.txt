You plan how a game gets built, the way a person builds one with an AI: in small steps, each adding one thing you can see on the screen when it is done. A developer builds the steps one after another on the same files. Each step is a fresh developer with {budget} turns who sees the files the earlier steps wrote and reads the design sections you name.

The game is the generic game of its genre, with no theme: every thing in it is a plain shape with its generic name written on or under it, in one default font, on a plain background. A theme is put on later by someone else; nothing here anticipates it.

The design is below: the concept (what the genre is and what is on the screen), what every game of the genre has, and how it is built.

The steps come in three parts, in this order.

1. The framework. The genre's core as the barest thing that plays, one piece at a time in the order the player meets them. Each step adds one thing that works and can be seen. No numbers are chosen for balance; plain ones.

2. What every one has. Every line of the list, one feature per step, or two small ones that belong together. Each step adds the feature whole and working: if the list says towers can be upgraded, after that step a player can upgrade a tower and see what changed. The screens the list names are steps here, the title screen first, and the last of them is "quit returns to the title screen".

3. Making it a game: the levels the list calls for, the rounds getting harder, the end that is won and the end that is lost, each on its own screen, and last a balance step that plays it and tunes the numbers until the early rounds are losable with nothing done and winnable with a few things done well.

Each step is small enough for its turns: one thing, and what it needs to be seen and checked. Write as many steps as the game needs, no fewer than twelve and no more than twenty-four.

For each step write:
- name: two to four words.
- stage: what to add, in one to four sentences, in plain words, concrete enough to build without guessing.
- done: one sentence on what a player sees or does when the step works.
- reads: the design's heading lines this step needs, copied exactly as they appear, including the leading # characters.

Answer with a JSON array of the steps with the keys "name", "stage", "done" and "reads", in a ```json block, and nothing after it.
