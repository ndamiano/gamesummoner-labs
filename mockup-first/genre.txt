Someone asked for this game:

{ask}

Do not design their game. Name the genre it belongs to, then describe the generic game of that
genre: the one every game of the genre is, before any theme is put on it. Write it the way a
person who has played a hundred of them would, as the list of things every one of them has.

The ask may name things the player does that the genre does not always have. Keep each one, as a
plain mechanic with no theme: what the player presses, what it does to what, and what that lets
the player do next. A game that drops what the ask says the player does is the wrong game.

Write exactly these headings and nothing else:

# 0. CONCEPT

One paragraph, 150 words at most. The genre by its common name. The view. What the player does,
turn by turn or second by second, the ask's own actions among them. What is on the screen. How it
is won and how it is lost. Name the kinds of things in it with the plainest generic words there
are (a tower, a walker, a path, a guard, an item): no theme, no setting, no name, no noun from
the ask.

# 1. WHAT EVERY ONE HAS

A list, one line each, of the features every game of this genre has and a player expects, and
the ask's own actions as features: the screens, the economy, the progression, the choices, the
feedback, the numbers (how many kinds, how many levels, how long a session). Each line is a thing
a player would miss if it were absent. Fifteen to twenty-five lines. No feature a game of this
genre does not need. A title screen, how to play in one screen or less, a win screen and a loss
screen are always lines.

Do not mention the ask's theme anywhere. The theme is put on later.
