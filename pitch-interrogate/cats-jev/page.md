# The pitcher's page

Ask: `a tower defense game where cats defend a fish market`

**Private page — The Fish Market Cats**  
This is for me only. Nobody else reads this.

I see the game as a wet dawn fish market, not a clean battlefield. The player is the Forecat, the organizer of the market. The game’s play is carried by building, combat, economy, waves, abilities, and scoring.

Opening minutes: the market is just opening. The player uses building to put cats at the gaps and angles of the market: a Mackerel Mouser on a crate, a Calico Counter Cat on a stall, an Alley Cat under the awning, a Ragdoll Rattler near the ice tubs. The player watches the opening waves come down the market aisles: seagulls testing from above, rats slipping between crates. The market earns through economy as customers buy fish and thieves are turned away. The player uses economy to add more cats and better positions.

What works against them: the market is exposed. Seagulls come from open sky. Rats slip through gaps. Later waves bring raccoons that take whole crates, and larger thieves that push through weak points. If the player leaves a gap, a thief steals fish, a customer leaves, economy tightens, and the next wave feels harsher. If the player spreads too thin, the cats are overwhelmed and the market loses stock. If enough waves break through, the market closes.

What happens when it goes wrong: it is not a simple collapse. A stolen fish becomes a darkened stall, a missing customer, a thinner market, and a hungrier wave. The player sees the market fail in its own objects: crates knocked open, ice melting, signs tilting, cat beds abandoned. Scoring records how badly the market was defended.

Still interesting after the opening minutes: waves change shape. A wave may send fast birds to distract while raccoons carry crates, or rats to draw cats away from the high-value stalls. The player must choose which cats to build, where economy should go, which abilities are worth it, and how to protect the market without leaving another opening. Scoring keeps the pressure clean: not just surviving, but defending the market well.

The particular things:

- Cats: the Forecat is the player’s role; defenders include Mackerel Mouser, Calico Counter Cat, Alley Cat, Ragdoll Rattler, and Tabby Bell Ringer.
- Enemies: Seagull thieves, Rat swarms, Raccoon crate thieves, and the Gull King.
- Objects: fish crates, ice tubs, awning poles, market scales, customer carts, cat beds, and a Closed sign.
- Jokes: cats flick mackerel at thieves, knock crates at the right moment, bat a fish tail into a gull’s path, and nap after a wave as if it were easy.
- Abilities: Yarn Ball Tangle, Catnip Nap, Hairball Frenzy, and Awning Drop.

What this game is not: not a cat raising game, not a fish cooking game, not a city builder, not an action game where you control a cat, not a generic tower defense with cats pasted onto human towers. It is a tower defense where cats defend a fish market, and the market is the target, the economy, and the stage.

**Pitch**

I’m pitching *The Fish Market Cats*. You’re the forecat of a dawn fish market. You use building to set cats at the stalls and crates, and combat turns those cats into the market’s defense as waves of seagulls, rats, and raccoons come for the catch. The market’s own fish trade feeds economy, abilities are quick cat-and-market tricks when a wave breaks through, and scoring records how much of the market you save. The game is plainly the one they asked for, but it is not generic: the market itself is under attack, and the catch feeds the defense.
