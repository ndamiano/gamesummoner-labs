Game prompt: a tower defense game where cats defend a fish market
the game: The player is the Forecat, organizer of a dawn fish market. The player builds cat beds at stalls, crates, aisles, and angles so cats automatically fight seagulls, rats, raccoons, and other thieves. Customers try to buy fish from open stalls; successful sales create coins, which buy more cats, repairs, and ability charges. Waves come down market aisles toward the Market Scale, crates, and exits; stolen fish and blocked customers darken stalls, abandon positions, lower economy, and make later waves harsher. Abilities are quick market tricks, and scoring records how well the market was defended, not merely whether it survived.
view: Fixed oblique overhead view of the fish market floor, showing market entrances, exits, aisles, stalls, fish crates, ice tubs, awning poles, Market Scale, customer carts, cat beds, and the Closed sign.
time: Real-time, with building and repair allowed before the first wave and after each wave is resolved, then the next wave begins when the market bell tolls the next count.
a run: The run starts by lifting the Closed sign and loading the Market Scale with the day’s stock. A wave breaks through when an enemy reaches the Market Scale or carries a fish crate to a market exit. The run ends when the Market Scale’s stock reaches zero and the Closed sign is hung. It becomes harder as lost stock and lost customers scale later waves, darkened stalls and abandoned cat beds create gaps and weak points, melting ice lowers sale value and attracts rats, and middle waves begin using distraction/draw-away patterns and larger thieves.
tone: “wet dawn fish market, not a clean battlefield”; “the market is the target, the economy, and the stage”; “Scoring keeps the pressure clean: not just surviving, but defending the market well”; failure is shown through “crates knocked open, ice melting, signs tilting, cat beds abandoned”.
look and sound: Wet dawn fish-market stalls, crates, awnings, ice, scales, carts, and cat beds; sounds of market bells, gulls, rats, raccoons, sales, cat tricks, and closing signs.

core mechanics:
- combat: A system of fighting between the player's side and enemies, where attacks deal damage until one side is defeated. In this game, the player does not directly control cats; placed cats automatically defend their covered area. Each cat has a defense area determined by its type and position; an area is covered when any placed cat covers it. Enemies enter at market entrances and move down the aisles toward the nearest unguarded stall, crate, or the Market Scale. Seagull thieves enter from the sky, fly over ground barriers, and target unguarded fish; if not stopped they steal one fish and leave. Rat swarms enter from drains and crates, move low, and nibble fish crates. Raccoon crate thieves pick up fish crates and carry them to an exit; they require sustained combat or knockback. Larger thieves are heavier variants that occupy weak points and cannot be turned away by a single small cat; they force the player to close gaps or lose stock. If an enemy enters an uncovered area, it steals fish from a crate or the Market Scale, nearby customers leave, that stall stops earning, and the next wave’s strength is scaled up by the lost stock and lost customers. If an enemy reaches the Market Scale or carries a fish crate to a market exit, the wave has broken through. A darkened stall stops customers from buying there and becomes a weak point. A knocked-open crate loses fish and gives rats a bonus. Melting ice reduces sale value and stock. A tilting sign warns that a gap is forming and makes customers avoid that path. An abandoned cat bed removes that building position and leaves the gap open until repaired. Cats are “overwhelmed” when an enemy enters a placed cat’s defense and the cat cannot turn it away before the enemy reaches a crate, customer, or the Market Scale; the cat retreats from its bed, the bed is abandoned, and the enemy proceeds to steal stock; the market loses fish or scale stock. The player has “spread too thin” when a defended area has no cat covering each enemy lane or high-value stall. A weak point is a darkened stall or other uncovered market position that larger thieves can occupy.
- building: A system where the player places pieces in the world to build structures of their own. In this game, the player uses economy to place purchasable cat beds at gaps and angles of the market. A gap is an aisle segment, stall, or cat bed whose approach is not covered by any placed cat’s defense. The player creates one by leaving that approach uncovered or losing a cat, and closes it by building a cat that covers it or by using an ability that blocks it. “Better positions” are places that cover more enemy aisles, protect high-value stalls or the Market Scale, and block exits; position quality is determined by how many enemy paths and gaps the cat covers, not by looks. A high-value stall is one with intact ice tubs, many fish crates, or close access to the Market Scale. Economy can build cat beds and repair cat beds, awning poles, ice tubs, and temporary market repairs. If a cat bed is abandoned, that position is lost until repaired. Temporary market repairs temporarily repair damaged market positions enough to restore function.
- waves: A system that sends threats at the player in rounds, each harder than the last. In this game, the first wave begins when the Closed sign is lifted and the Market Scale is loaded with the day’s stock. Later waves begin after the previous wave is resolved and the market bell tolls the next count. A wave ends when every enemy is defeated or has left through a market exit carrying stolen fish. Waves choose patterns after the market has defended high-value stalls, usually from the middle waves onward. Fast birds are seagull scouts that enter near anti-air cats; “distract” means those cats spend their combat time on the birds while raccoons move toward crates. Rats “draw cats away” by entering near high-value stalls so ground cats target them instead of heavier thieves behind them. A hungrier wave adds more enemies or moves them faster. Larger thieves occupy weak points and cannot be turned away by a single small cat; they force the player to close gaps or lose stock. The Gull King is a special boss wave that drops from an awning, targets the Market Scale, summons seagulls, and tries to carry a whole crate; if it reaches the Market Scale, it causes an immediate closing blow. An immediate closing blow is a large immediate loss to Market Scale stock; if this lowers stock to zero, the market closes.
- economy: A system where the player earns a currency as they play and spends it on things that help them. In this game, customers are market patrons who walk in from the street and try to reach an open stall. A sale happens when the aisle is not occupied by an enemy, the stall’s fish crate is intact, and no thief is currently taking that fish. If combat turns the thief away first, the customer buys and economy gains coins; if the customer is blocked or the stall is darkened, the customer leaves and the sale is lost. Customer carts matter when customers arrive; if an enemy blocks one, the customer leaves, and if the sale completes, economy gains. The market earns through economy as customers buy fish and thieves are turned away. Fish crates and ice tubs feed the Market Scale’s stock; the Market Scale is the stock meter. A missing customer lowers the customer count, reduces future earning, and feeds the next wave’s hunger. A thinner market means fewer customers and lower economy. Economy buys cat beds, repairs to cat beds, awning poles, and ice tubs, ability charges, and temporary market repairs.

supporting mechanics:
- abilities: A system of special powers beyond the player's basic actions, each triggered by its own input and limited by a cooldown. In this game, each ability is obtained by spending economy to charge a market tool; it is available once charged and off cooldown. Abilities are quick cat-and-market tricks used when a wave breaks through or a gap appears. Yarn Ball Tangle stops a line of enemies in an aisle for a short time. Catnip Nap puts enemies near a cat bed to sleep and cancels their theft actions. Hairball Frenzy makes nearby cats attack faster for a short time. Awning Drop uses an intact awning pole to crush enemies below and temporarily block a gap. If an ability has no valid target or area, it fizzles and its cooldown begins.
- scoring: A system that awards points for how well the player plays and records the best scores. In this game, scoring records stock saved, coins earned, customers served, enemies defeated, and stalls left unbroken. It awards points for defending high-value stalls, keeping aisles covered, and ending waves without lost stock. It records badly for customers left, darkened stalls, stolen crates, melted ice, abandoned cat beds, and a closed market. Scoring is recorded after the wave ends.
