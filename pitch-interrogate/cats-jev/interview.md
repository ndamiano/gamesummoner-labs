### Pitcher

1. In my game, “the market” is the Market Scale and the fish crates and ice tubs that feed it. A wave breaks through when an enemy reaches the Market Scale or carries a fish crate to a market exit. The run ends when the Closed sign is hung because the Market Scale’s stock reaches zero.

2. Customers are market patrons who walk in from the street and try to reach an open stall. A sale happens when the aisle is not occupied by an enemy, the stall’s fish crate is intact, and no thief is currently taking that fish. If combat turns the thief away first, the customer buys and economy gains coins; if the customer is blocked or the stall is darkened, the customer leaves and the sale is lost.

3. The first wave begins when the Closed sign is lifted and the Market Scale is loaded with the day’s stock. Later waves begin after the previous wave is resolved and the market bell tolls the next count. A wave ends when every enemy is defeated or has left through a market exit carrying stolen fish. Enemies enter at market entrances and move down the aisles toward the nearest unguarded stall, crate, or the Market Scale.

4. A gap is an aisle segment, stall, or cat bed whose approach is not covered by any placed cat’s defense. The player creates one by leaving that approach uncovered or losing a cat, and closes it by building a cat that covers it or by using an ability that blocks it. When an enemy enters an uncovered area, it steals fish from a crate or the Market Scale, nearby customers leave, that stall stops earning, and the next wave’s strength is scaled up by the lost stock and lost customers.

5. Economy makes each cat available as a purchasable cat bed. Mackerel Mouser is a cheap opener that attacks ground thieves passing in front of it. Calico Counter Cat is costlier and targets seagulls and airborne thieves from a counter. Alley Cat is a cheap ground cat that attacks enemies passing under its stall or awning and slows rats. Ragdoll Rattler is a heavier cost cat that knocks back crate thieves and stuns raccoons. Tabby Bell Ringer is an expensive support cat that rings a bell to make nearby thieves hesitate and helps nearby cats focus the nearest thief.

6. Seagull thieves enter from the sky, fly over ground barriers, and target unguarded fish; if not stopped they steal one fish and leave. Rat swarms enter from drains and crates, move low, and nibble fish crates; they are stopped by ground cats and abilities. Raccoon crate thieves are heavy ground thieves that pick up fish crates and carry them to an exit; they require sustained combat or knockback. The Gull King is a special boss wave that drops from an awning, targets the Market Scale, summons seagulls, and tries to carry a whole crate; if it reaches the Market Scale, it causes an immediate closing blow. Larger thieves are heavier variants that occupy weak points and cannot be turned away by a single small cat; they force the player to close gaps or lose stock.

7. Each ability is obtained by spending economy to charge a market tool; it is available once charged and off cooldown. Yarn Ball Tangle stops a line of enemies in an aisle for a short time. Catnip Nap puts enemies near a cat bed to sleep and cancels their theft actions. Hairball Frenzy makes nearby cats attack faster for a short time. Awning Drop uses an intact awning pole to crush enemies below and temporarily block a gap. If an ability has no valid target or area, it fizzles and its cooldown begins.

8. Fish crates matter from opening: they hold stock, are bought from, and are stolen. Ice tubs matter when customers or enemies first touch them; intact ice preserves stock and improves sales, while melting lowers stock value and attracts rats. Awning poles matter when Awning Drop or the Gull King touches them; if damaged, they create a gap and reduce customer flow. Market scales matter when an enemy reaches the market heart; they are the defended target and the stock meter. Customer carts matter when customers arrive; if an enemy blocks one, the customer leaves, and if the sale completes, economy gains. Cat beds matter when building cats; if abandoned, that position is lost until repaired. The Closed sign appears only when the run ends.

9. Scoring records stock saved, coins earned, customers served, enemies defeated, and stalls left unbroken. It awards points for defending high-value stalls, keeping aisles covered, and ending waves without lost stock. It records badly for customers left, darkened stalls, stolen crates, melted ice, abandoned cat beds, and a closed market.

10. A darkened stall stops customers from buying there and becomes a weak point. A missing customer lowers the customer count, reduces future earning, and feeds the next wave’s hunger. A thinner market means fewer customers and lower economy. A hungrier wave adds more enemies or moves them faster. A knocked-open crate loses fish and gives rats a bonus. Melting ice reduces sale value and stock. A tilting sign warns that a gap is forming and makes customers avoid that path. An abandoned cat bed removes that building position and leaves the gap open until repaired.

11. The player has “spread too thin” when a defended area has no cat covering each enemy lane or high-value stall. Cats are “overwhelmed” when an enemy enters a placed cat’s defense and the cat cannot turn it away before the enemy reaches a crate, customer, or the Market Scale. When that happens, the cat retreats from its bed, the bed is abandoned, and the enemy proceeds to steal stock; the market loses fish or scale stock.

12. Waves choose these patterns after the market has defended high-value stalls, usually from the middle waves onward. Fast birds are seagull scouts that enter near anti-air cats; “distract” means those cats spend their combat time on the birds while raccoons move toward crates. Rats “draw cats away” by entering near high-value stalls so ground cats target them instead of heavier thieves behind them. A high-value stall is one with intact ice tubs, many fish crates, or close access to the Market Scale.

13. Economy buys cat beds, repairs to cat beds, awning poles, and ice tubs, ability charges, and temporary market repairs. “Better positions” are places that cover more enemy aisles, protect high-value stalls or the Market Scale, and block exits; position quality is determined by how many enemy paths and gaps the cat covers, not by looks.

14. The jokes are automatic feedback, not player actions. A mackerel flick happens when a counter cat defeats an airborne thief; a crate knock happens when an ability or Rattler affects enemies near crates; a fish-tail bat happens when a tangle or nap affects enemies near fish; a post-wave nap happens after the wave ends and scoring is recorded. They tell the player what happened and add flavor, but they do not create separate rules.
