# The interview

## Round 1

### Interrogator

CRACKS
- The page’s later interest rests on “scoring rewards clean, complete crossings” and “leaving a better score than before,” but it does not say what a crossing is, what makes one clean or complete, what score is being left behind, or when it is awarded.
- The page says “A collectible is visible but not reachable on the straight line” and “hidden collectibles tucked behind hazard arrangements,” but it does not say how the player finds or reaches those collectibles or alternate routes, whether they are visible or unseen, or what obtaining one changes.
- The page says “the attempt returns to the last safe edge” after a bad crossing or hazard touch, but it does not define the last safe edge or say what persists after failure, such as collected moths, score, or room progress.
- The page says the game expands into “connected swamp rooms” and lists later rooms, but it does not say what ends the opening room, what starts another room, or what completes the sequence of rooms described after the opening stretch.

QUESTIONS
1. What is a “crossing” in game terms, and what makes one “clean” and “complete” enough to produce “a better score than before”?
2. How does the player discover and reach a “collectible ... not reachable on the straight line,” a “hidden collectibles tucked behind hazard arrangements,” or an “alternate route,” and what changes when the player succeeds?
3. When “the attempt returns to the last safe edge,” what is that edge, and what is preserved or reset after the failure?
4. What rule lets the player leave the opening room for another room, and what rule marks completion of the room sequence the page describes after the opening stretch?

### Pitcher

1. In my game, a crossing is one attempt from a room’s start edge to its exit edge. It is complete when the frog reaches the exit edge. It is clean when no hazard is touched and no fall returns the frog. The recorded score is the room’s best completed clean crossing: reaching the exit, doing it clean, and collecting moths in that room can raise it.

2. The player discovers moths by taking platforming lines that look past the obvious path. A moth may be visible but placed where the straight line cannot reach it, behind hazards or on a longer ledge route. It is reached by an alternate platforming line. When collected, it counts toward the set and adds to the room’s recorded score; it gives no new ability and does not change the room.

3. The last safe edge is the nearest non-hazard surface the frog was standing on before the failed move. If there is none in the current attempt, it is the room’s start edge. After failure, the frog returns there and the current attempt’s unrecorded crossing progress is discarded. Collected moths, completed rooms, and recorded best scores remain.

4. The player leaves a room by completing its crossing to its exit edge, such as the drain at the end of the opening greenhouse. That exit opens the next connected room. The described room sequence is complete when the player has reached and exited the abandoned children’s pond, the last room named on the page.
