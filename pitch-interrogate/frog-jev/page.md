# The pitcher's page

Ask: `a platformer where you play as a frog`

**Page for myself**

Title: **Bullfrog Crossing**

Opening minutes: The screen opens on a rain-swollen greenhouse after closing. The player is a small frog sitting on a cracked tile edge, with no instruction except the shape of the next safe surface. The opening room asks for platforming across overturned pots, wet glass, and lily pads floating in a flooded basin. The player reads the space, then uses platforming to get from one safe surface to another. The earliest hazards are placed as obvious, fixed obstacles: a shard of broken glass, a slick of black moss, a snapped mousetrap waiting in a leaf. The player learns that touching them breaks the crossing. A collectible is visible but not reachable on the straight line: a pale moth behind a pot. The player is pulled toward platforming, hazards, and the hidden collectible.

What is working against them: the space is narrow, wet, and dark. The frog cannot simply pass through; every surface change demands platforming, and hazards are arranged to punish careless lines. The greenhouse is full of small traps and bad edges. The player’s attention is split between the immediate crossing, the hidden collectible, and the temptation to take a faster route.

When it goes wrong: a bad crossing leaves the frog in the water, and the attempt returns to the last safe edge. A hazard touch does the same. The game does not fight back. It takes the rhythm away. The player sees the line they failed and tries another one.

What is still interesting after the opening stretch: the greenhouse expands into connected swamp rooms: a flooded potting bench, a broken fountain, a root-choked greenhouse, an abandoned children’s pond. Each room has hidden collectibles tucked behind hazard arrangements, and scoring rewards clean, complete crossings. The player starts to care about unseen collectibles, alternate routes, and the score they can leave behind.

Particular things: pale moths as collectibles; moonlit glass; flooded clay pots; lily pads in a greenhouse basin; fixed hazards like snapped mousetraps, black moss, broken glass, and still pools of oil; a frog that belongs in the swamp but has been displaced into a human greenhouse.

What this game is not: it is not a combat game, not a shooter, not a puzzle game, not a frog with special powers, not an open world, not a game about new verbs. It is not about fighting the greenhouse. It is about crossing it.

**Pitch**

Bullfrog Crossing is a frog platformer set in a moonlit greenhouse that has become a swamp. You play as a small frog using platforming across flooded pots, glass shelves, and lily pads, while hazards like black moss, broken glass, and oil make every crossing feel risky. You are after the collectibles hidden in the dark and the scoring you can leave behind by completing the crossings cleanly. The game is this particular quiet, rainy world, where the pleasure is finding the safe line through a dangerous place and leaving a better score than before.
