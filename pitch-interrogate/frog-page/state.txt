Game prompt: a platformer where you play as a frog

the game: You play as a small frog trying to get to the moonlit pond on the rooftops, where the other frogs are already calling, moving through a wet world of rain barrels, gutters, garden edges, wet walls, branches, twigs, and other small physical supports. The player does not run or fight; the frog moves by crouching to coil and aim a committed leap, sticking to wet surfaces, hanging from twigs, and swinging with its tongue from catchable anchors. The tongue is primarily for anchoring, hanging, swinging, and catching grabbable supports, and it can also disturb or pull small physical objects when that changes the scene physically, such as disturbing a branch, pulling a cork, or catching a strand of spider silk. The game is built as crafted levels or legs of the journey: each leg moves the frog from a low, exposed place to a higher safe perch or district boundary, while the moonlit pond and the calling frogs remain the distant goal. Difficulty comes from reading the place like a frog: what can be caught, what will break, what is too slick, and what is watching the route. Watching dangers such as cats, herons, fish, sprinklers, and similar hazards are readable through attention or movement patterns and occupy tempting paths, so the player avoids them, waits, distracts them through physical cause and effect, or takes a harder physical route. A bad jump cannot be casually corrected in midair; the frog may slide, fall, or dangle for a beat before rethinking, and failure returns the player to the last safe perch or the leg start. The first day of the game is exactly the named verbs and material cues: crouch, committed leap, stick, hang, swing, grabbable supports, wet/slippery/breakable surfaces, and at least one readable watching danger.

view: side-scrolling 2D platformer view with the camera keeping the frog, the next perch, supports, surfaces, and watching dangers in frame

time: real-time

a run: A run is one crafted leg of the journey. It starts at a low, exposed place and ends when the frog reaches a higher safe perch or district boundary. Failure returns the frog to the last safe perch, or to the leg start if it has not reached one yet. The moonlit pond and the calling frogs remain the distant goal. Later legs raise difficulty by making the obvious path watched, fragile, slick, or broken, and by making the clever path demand longer, riskier physical chains.

tone: small, slippery, and alive in a wet world; the joy is finding the clever, physical route upward when the obvious one would get you swallowed; getting caught is embarrassment and reset, not combat.

look and sound: moonlit wet rooftops and garden edges, with soft frog calls, water sounds, and hazards readable through attention and movement.

core mechanics:
- crouch and committed leap: crouch is the coil that lets the player set up and aim a committed takeoff; it is not a stealth posture. The player plans and commits to short upward routes chaining crouch, leap, stick, hang, and swing. Once the frog leaves the ground it cannot casually correct or freely steer; a bad jump can mean sliding, falling, or hanging for a beat before rethinking.
- tongue anchoring and swinging: the tongue is primarily for anchoring, hanging, swinging, and catching grabbable supports. Sturdy edges, branches, or grabbable anchors can be swung from only if they are actually catchable. The tongue is not for attacking enemies or filling a collectathon.
- stick, slide, and surface state: grip and wetness are the language of the level. Wet walls can be stuck to. Smooth or dry surfaces are too slick. Landing or moving on a bad surface can mean sliding off or losing the perch.
- breakable supports: thin supports can break under weight or a bad tug. The player must read what will break, especially when hanging, swinging, or tugging something.
- watching dangers and attention: watching dangers occupy tempting routes and punish careless paths. Cats, herons, fish, sprinklers, and similar hazards are readable through attention or movement patterns. The player avoids, waits, or takes a harder route. Avoiding attention comes from stillness, cover, and timing, not from entering a special hidden crouch state.
- failure, dangle, and reset: the frog can sometimes catch a late edge, slide, or dangle for a beat after a bad jump, but it never gets free midair steering. Failure usually means losing the perch and having to rethink the route. Getting caught is embarrassment and reset, not combat. Failure returns the frog to the last safe perch, or to the leg start if it has not reached one yet.

supporting mechanics:
- tongue physical disturbance: the tongue may grab and pull small physical objects if that changes the scene physically, like disturbing a branch, pulling a cork, or catching a strand of spider silk. This can make a hazard react, be delayed, or reveal a route through physical cause and effect; the frog still cannot fight or command the danger directly.
- crafted leg progression and difficulty escalation: each leg moves the frog from a low, exposed place to a higher safe perch or new district. Later levels raise difficulty by making the obvious path watched, fragile, slick, or broken, and the clever path demand longer, riskier physical chains.
