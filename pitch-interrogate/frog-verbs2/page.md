# The pitcher's page

Ask: `a platformer where you play as a frog`

## Page for myself

**Title:** *Moon Pool*

The systems this game is made of are **platforming**, **hazards**, **stealth**, **time cycle**, and **collectibles**.

What I see:

A small tree frog wakes in a rain barrel in a crowded rooftop garden. The Moon Pool behind the old greenhouse is going to be drained when the first light comes, so the frog has to cross from garden to garden and reach it.

First moments:

The frog is on a lily in a rain barrel. A gutter runs to a windowsill, and beyond that a wire leads to an awning. The air is wet and loud. A drain steams under the gutter. A heron stands in the yard, not looking toward the frog. The frog waits in shadow, moves when the yard is quiet, and slips past the drain. A rain lily bulb glints under a loose tile. The frog takes it. Then a cat appears on the awning, and the safe route becomes a choice: wait, or risk being seen.

What is working against the frog:

The watchers in the garden. Fixed dangers: drains, lamps, broken glass, fans, open gutters. The changing night. The fact that the frog is small and exposed.

When it goes wrong:

If a watcher notices the frog, the watcher turns and the way is no longer there. The frog has to hide until another way appears. If a hazard takes the frog from the path, the crossing is lost and the path must be tried again. If the night changes too far, the Moon Pool is closed for the attempt.

What remains interesting after a while:

The player learns which routes are quiet and which are worth the risk. The same gardens change as the night moves: watchers shift, lights change, hidden places become reachable or deadly. Rain lily bulbs are hidden throughout the gardens as a set, and the game keeps asking whether to collect them or go straight for the Moon Pool.

Particular things:

Rain lily bulbs, the Moon Pool, rain barrels, gutters, awnings, greenhouse glass, a heron, a cat, drains, lamps, wet ledges, loose tiles, shadow.

What this game is not:

It is not a road-crossing game, not a combat game, not a story game with named characters, not a survival game, not a roguelike, not a game about vehicles, and not a game where the player builds or fights through the world.

## Pitch

You play a small tree frog trying to cross a rain-soaked rooftop garden to the Moon Pool before dawn. You move through wet gutters, awnings, and ledges, waiting in shadow until the heron looks away, threading past drains and lamps, and finding rain lily bulbs hidden in the corners. The game is about being small in a place that notices you: the night changes around you, and every crossing is a decision about whether to be seen.
